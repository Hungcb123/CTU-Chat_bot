\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

%------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Architectural Findings and Operational Trade-offs}

\textbf{RQ1 (Architecture-Level Effectiveness):}
The tested orchestration bundle achieves a higher automated and semantic pass rate point estimate alongside fewer specialist input tokens than Single Agent. Fact Coverage confidence intervals span zero, and latency measures different pipeline stages; neither completeness nor speed advantage follows. The No Specialist Policy ablation confirms domain instructions contribute meaningfully under evaluated conditions.

\textbf{RQ2 \& RQ3 (Mechanisms, Tool Isolation, Registry Scaling):}
Ablations associate specialist policies and heterogeneous evidence access with the largest pass-rate gains. Disabling deterministic calculators yields an estimated $-4.0$\,pp change in overall fact pass (95\% CI $[-9.0, 0.0]$\,pp); without route repair, cross-domain pass falls from 80.0\% to 65.0\%. At 11 tools, removing isolation increases input tokens without altering pass rates. At 51 tools, Routed Specialist achieves 88.0\% tool E2E Success, $9.3$\,pp above Global Top-10 (CI $[+2.0, +17.3]$\,pp; $+4.7$\,pp over Full-Registry Single Agent, CI $[-1.3, +11.3]$\,pp), supporting bounded tool exposure under ambiguity.

\textbf{Operational Trade-offs and Deployment Guidance:}
Specialist execution records $1{,}731$\,ms and $677$ output tokens more than generic execution. A single agent suits narrow tasks with small tool pools, whereas scoped specialists suit institutions with distinct evidence formats and fee calculations. Adapting \system{} elsewhere requires mapping curriculum schemas, loading policies into stores, encoding fee rules in deterministic tools, and updating intent prompts.

%------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Relation to Prior Work}

ReAct~\cite{yao2023react} and Toolformer~\cite{schick2023toolformer} study tool use; AutoGen~\cite{wu2023autogen} supports agent coordination. \system{} instead routes to bounded specialists with evidence paths chosen by data modality, differing from Adaptive-RAG's complexity-based retrieval~\cite{jeong2024adaptiverag}. The Source Recall drop under Uniform Text-RAG aligns with GraphRAG's rationale for relational evidence~\cite{edge2024graphrag}. These frameworks were not directly compared in our experiments.

%------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limitations}
\textbf{Internal and operational:} Pipeline latency ($10.65$\,s) limits interactive use; differing measurement boundaries prevent direct speed rankings. \textbf{External:} Single-institution evaluation; transfer to other curricula and fee rules remains untested. Redis history was excluded, leaving multi-turn effects unmeasured. \textbf{Construct:} Lexical matching can pass invalid financial outputs (e.g., \texttt{HOUT-DIR-FIN-03} scored 50\% despite a 192.183M vs.\ 40M VND error). Our LLM judge and Numeric Exact Match strictly penalized this ($\text{AC}=0.10, \text{Num-EM}=0.0$), validating that deterministic calculators provide superior financial precision (62.5\% vs.\ 60.0\%). \textbf{Statistical:} Paired analysis uses 10,000 bootstrap resamples; sub-family subsets have smaller samples ($n=19$--$33$).
