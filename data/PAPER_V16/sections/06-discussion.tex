\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

%------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Architectural Findings and Operational Trade-offs}

\textbf{RQ1 (Architecture-Level Effectiveness):}
\system{} achieves higher automated and semantic pass estimates with fewer specialist input tokens than Single Agent. Fact Coverage CIs span zero, and latency measures differing pipeline stages; neither completeness nor speed advantage follows. Ablating specialist policies confirms domain instructions contribute under evaluated conditions.

\textbf{RQ2 \& RQ3 (Mechanisms, Tool Isolation, Registry Scaling):}
Specialist policies and heterogeneous evidence drive largest gains. Ablating deterministic calculators lowers fact pass by an estimated $-4.0$\,pp (95\% CI $[-9.0, 0.0]$\,pp); without route repair, cross-domain pass drops from 80.0\% to 65.0\%. At 11 tools, removing isolation increases input tokens without altering pass rates. At 51 tools, Routed Specialist achieves 88.0\% tool E2E Success ($+9.3$\,pp over Global Top-10, CI $[+2.0, +17.3]$\,pp; $+4.7$\,pp over Single Agent, CI $[-1.3, +11.3]$\,pp), validating bounded tool exposure under ambiguity.

\textbf{Operational Trade-offs and Deployment Guidance:}
(1)~\emph{When to choose Single Agent:} Optimal for homogeneous text regulations without multi-hop prerequisites. Bypassing supervisor routing yields lower latency (${\sim}5.2$\,s vs.\ $10.65$\,s end-to-end), saves ${\sim}650$ routing tokens, and achieves higher Answer Relevancy (80.9\% vs.\ 77.8\%) via concise, direct answers without extraneous counseling details. It remains dependable when tool pools are small ($\le 11$ tools, 94.0\% selection accuracy).
(2)~\emph{When to adopt \system{}:} Essential when queries require heterogeneous evidence modalities (Neo4j graphs, vector search, deterministic fee calculators) or span cross-domain rules (80.0\% vs.\ 70.0\% pass). For high-stakes queries where calculation errors carry penalties, \system{} delivers superior strict semantic correctness ($\text{Pass}_{\ge 75\%}^{\text{Jdg}}$: 37.0\% vs.\ 33.0\%) and exact financial accuracy ($\text{Num-EM}$: 62.5\% vs.\ 60.0\%). When scaling tool registries ($\ge 50$ tools), scoped specialist isolation is vital to avoid selection failure (88.0\% vs.\ 78.7\% E2E). Institutional transfer requires mapping schemas, loading stores, encoding fee rules, and calibrating routing prompts.

%------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Relation to Prior Work}

While ReAct~\cite{yao2023react}, Toolformer~\cite{schick2023toolformer}, and AutoGen~\cite{wu2023autogen} examine generic tool and agent coordination, \system{} routes queries to bounded specialists with modality-tailored evidence paths. Unlike Adaptive-RAG's complexity-driven retrieval~\cite{jeong2024adaptiverag}, our approach isolates specialist registries, aligning with GraphRAG's findings on the necessity of relational evidence~\cite{edge2024graphrag}.

%------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limitations}
\textbf{Internal/operational:} $10.65$\,s latency limits interactivity; differing boundaries prevent direct speed rankings. \textbf{External:} Single-institution evaluation; transfer remains untested. Multi-turn history was omitted. \textbf{Construct:} Lexical matching can pass invalid financial outputs (e.g., \texttt{HOUT-DIR-FIN-03} scored 50\% despite a 192.183M vs.\ 40M VND error). Our LLM judge and Numeric Exact Match strictly penalized this ($\text{AC}=0.10, \text{Num-EM}=0.0$), confirming deterministic calculators deliver superior financial precision (62.5\% vs.\ 60.0\%). \textbf{Statistical:} Paired bootstrap analysis ($N{=}100$, 10,000 resamples); subsets have smaller samples ($n=19$--$33$).
