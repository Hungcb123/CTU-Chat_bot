\subsection{Architectural Findings and Operational Trade-offs}

\textbf{Architectural Findings (RQ1--RQ3):}
\system{} achieves higher lexical pass (+10.0\,pp) and uses 41.4\% fewer specialist input tokens than Single Agent. Ablations confirm that specialist policies and heterogeneous evidence drive the largest gains. Ablating deterministic calculators lowers fact pass by an estimated $-4.0$\,pp (95\% CI $[-9.0, 0.0]$\,pp); without route repair, cross-domain pass drops from 80.0\% to 65.0\%. At 11 tools, removing isolation increases input tokens without altering pass rates. At 51 tools, Routed Specialist achieves 88.0\% tool E2E Success ($+9.3$\,pp over Top-10, CI $[+2.0, +17.3]$\,pp; $+4.7$\,pp over Single Agent, CI $[-1.3, +11.3]$\,pp), demonstrating robustness under ambiguity.

\textbf{Deployment Guidance and Trade-offs:}
(1)~\emph{Single Agent} is preferred for homogeneous text policies without multi-hop prerequisites: bypassing supervisor routing reduces latency (${\sim}5.2$\,s vs.\ $10.65$\,s), saves ${\sim}650$ routing tokens, and achieves higher Answer Relevancy (80.9\% vs.\ 77.8\%) via concise answers without extraneous counseling context. It remains dependable for small tool pools ($\le 11$ tools, 94.0\% selection).
(2)~\emph{\system{}} is recommended when queries involve heterogeneous representations (graphs, vector search, calculators) or cross-domain rules (80.0\% vs.\ 70.0\% pass). For high-stakes financial queries, it mitigates arithmetic hallucination, yielding higher strict semantic pass ($\text{Pass}_{\ge 75\%}^{\text{Jdg}}$: 37.0\% vs.\ 33.0\%, paired McNemar $p{=}0.4807$, 95\% CI $[-4.0, +12.0]$\,pp) and numeric match ($\text{Num-EM}$: 62.5\% vs.\ 60.0\%, 95\% CI $[-6.7, +11.8]$\,pp), though both intervals span zero. Under registry scaling ($\ge 50$ tools), specialist scoping prevents selection collapse (88.0\% vs.\ 78.7\% E2E). Institutional transfer requires schema mapping, rule encoding, and routing calibration.

\paragraph{Relation to Prior Work.}
While ReAct~\cite{yao2023react}, Toolformer~\cite{schick2023toolformer}, and AutoGen~\cite{wu2023autogen} examine generic coordination, \system{} routes queries to bounded specialists with modality-tailored evidence paths. Unlike Adaptive-RAG's complexity retrieval~\cite{jeong2024adaptiverag}, our approach isolates specialist registries, aligning with GraphRAG on relational evidence~\cite{edge2024graphrag}.

\paragraph{Threats to Validity.}
\emph{Internal/operational:} $10.65$\,s latency limits interactivity; differing boundaries prevent direct speed rankings. \emph{External:} Single-institution evaluation; transfer remains untested. Multi-turn history was omitted. \emph{Construct:} Lexical matching can pass invalid outputs (e.g., \texttt{HOUT-DIR-FIN-03} scored 50\% despite a 192.183M vs.\ 40M VND error); programmatic evaluation penalized this ($\text{AC}=0.10, \text{Num-EM}=0.0$), catching arithmetic errors that substring overlap forgives ($\text{Num-EM}$: 62.5\% vs.\ 60.0\%). \emph{Statistical:} Paired bootstrap analysis ($N{=}100$, 10,000 resamples); subsets have smaller samples ($n=19$--$33$).
