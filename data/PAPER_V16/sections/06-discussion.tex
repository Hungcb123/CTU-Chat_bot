\section{Discussion}

%------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Architectural Findings and Operational Trade-offs}

\textbf{RQ1 (Architecture-Level Effectiveness):}
\system{} achieves higher lexical pass estimates (+10.0\,pp) and uses 41.4\% fewer specialist input tokens than Single Agent. While Fact Coverage CIs span zero, ablating specialist policies confirms that domain-scoped instructions contribute to response reliability under evaluated conditions.

\textbf{RQ2 \& RQ3 (Mechanisms, Tool Isolation, Registry Scaling):}
Specialist policies and heterogeneous evidence drive largest gains. Ablating deterministic calculators lowers fact pass by an estimated $-4.0$\,pp (95\% CI $[-9.0, 0.0]$\,pp); without route repair, cross-domain pass drops from 80.0\% to 65.0\%. At 11 tools, removing isolation increases input tokens without altering pass rates. At 51 tools, Routed Specialist achieves 88.0\% tool E2E Success ($+9.3$\,pp over Global Top-10, CI $[+2.0, +17.3]$\,pp; $+4.7$\,pp over Single Agent, CI $[-1.3, +11.3]$\,pp), validating bounded tool exposure under ambiguity.

\textbf{Operational Trade-offs and Deployment Guidance:}
(1)~\emph{When to choose Single Agent:} Optimal for homogeneous text regulations without multi-hop prerequisites. Bypassing supervisor routing yields lower latency (${\sim}5.2$\,s vs.\ $10.65$\,s), saves ${\sim}650$ routing tokens, and achieves higher Answer Relevancy (80.9\% vs.\ 77.8\%) via direct answers without broad counseling context. It remains dependable when tool pools are small ($\le 11$ tools, 94.0\% selection accuracy).
(2)~\emph{When to adopt \system{}:} Recommended when queries require heterogeneous modalities (Neo4j graphs, vector search, deterministic fee calculators) or span cross-domain rules (80.0\% vs.\ 70.0\% pass). For high-stakes queries with financial penalties, \system{} mitigates arithmetic hallucination, yielding higher point estimates on strict semantic pass ($\text{Pass}_{\ge 75\%}^{\text{Jdg}}$: 37.0\% vs.\ 33.0\%, paired McNemar $p{=}0.4807$, 95\% CI $[-4.0, +12.0]$\,pp) and numeric match ($\text{Num-EM}$: 62.5\% vs.\ 60.0\%, 95\% CI $[-6.7, +11.8]$\,pp), though both intervals span zero. Under registry expansion ($\ge 50$ tools), specialist isolation prevents selection collapse (88.0\% vs.\ 78.7\% E2E). Institutional transfer requires schema mapping, rule encoding, and routing prompt calibration.

\subsection{Relation to Prior Work}

While ReAct~\cite{yao2023react}, Toolformer~\cite{schick2023toolformer}, and AutoGen~\cite{wu2023autogen} examine generic tool and agent coordination, \system{} routes queries to bounded specialists with modality-tailored evidence paths. Unlike Adaptive-RAG's complexity-driven retrieval~\cite{jeong2024adaptiverag}, our approach isolates specialist registries, aligning with GraphRAG's findings on the necessity of relational evidence~\cite{edge2024graphrag}.

%------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limitations}
\textbf{Internal/operational:} $10.65$\,s latency limits interactivity; differing boundaries prevent direct speed rankings. \textbf{External:} Single-institution evaluation; transfer remains untested. Multi-turn history was omitted. \textbf{Construct:} Lexical matching can pass invalid financial outputs (e.g., \texttt{HOUT-DIR-FIN-03} scored 50\% despite a 192.183M vs.\ 40M VND error). Our LLM judge and Numeric Exact Match strictly penalized this ($\text{AC}=0.10, \text{Num-EM}=0.0$), showing that programmatic evaluation catches arithmetic errors that substring overlap forgives ($\text{Num-EM}$: 62.5\% vs.\ 60.0\%). \textbf{Statistical:} Paired bootstrap analysis ($N{=}100$, 10,000 resamples); subsets have smaller samples ($n=19$--$33$).
