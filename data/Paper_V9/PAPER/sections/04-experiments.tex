\section{Experimental Results}
\label{sec:exp}

This section presents the empirical evaluation of \system{}.

%------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Experimental Benchmark and Protocols}
\label{sec:dataset}

We evaluate \system{} on 200 administrative queries constructed through a document-grounded semi-automated protocol over official Can Tho University (CTU) statutes. Candidate queries with diverse conversational forms were generated from statutory articles and reviewed for ground-truth intents, passages, required facts, and answers. The 100-query development split supports index calibration, prompt engineering, and failure analysis. The separately constructed 100-query held-out set follows a predefined complexity distribution of 40 direct single-hop, 20 within-domain multi-hop, 20 cross-domain, 10 entity-comparison, 5 temporal, and 5 adversarial queries. It includes 51 formal and 49 colloquial formulations; 56 queries have one gold source and 44 require multiple gold sources.

An audit using entity inventories separate from the development split found no exact cross-split duplicates and a maximum 5-gram Jaccard similarity of 0.1875. Gold sources and required facts were reviewed against 244 institutional documents and 559 structured tuition records. The held-out set contains 19 Academic, 28 Financial, 17 Scholarship, and 16 General single-domain queries; the remaining 20 combine evidence across domains and are analyzed separately. Tables~\ref{tab:retrieval_evaluation} and~\ref{tab:e2e_evaluation} report results on this held-out set.

After evidence review, 27 reference answers and 26 required-fact lists were corrected without changing questions, gold sources, domains, or complexity labels. Ragas observations for the reviewed cases were re-evaluated, and the final reporting audit recomputed Fact EM deterministically against the revised required facts.

\noindent\textbf{Evaluated Snapshot and Infrastructure:} Reported results use a fixed Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite snapshot through Google Cloud Vertex AI with greedy decoding ($T=0.0$); later deployment-model substitutions are not evaluated. Each query was run $R=3$ times, yielding $2,100$ generation and Ragas turns ($7$ configurations $\times 100$ queries $\times 3$ runs). Neural reranking used an NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1650 GPU (4~GB VRAM). Neo4j 5.20 with APOC stored relational curricula; PostgreSQL stored parent chunks (2,800 characters, 100 overlap); and Qdrant indexed child chunks (400 characters, 50 overlap) using a 768-dimensional \texttt{vietnamese-bi-encoder}. The system used \texttt{bge-reranker-v2-m3}, LangGraph StateGraph, and Redis 7.

%------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}

\noindent\textbf{Evaluation Metrics:} Scenario~1 reports source-document-level Hit@$k$ ($k\in\{1,3\}$), Precision@5, Recall@5, MRR@10, and domain-level Hit@1. Scenario~3 measures routing accuracy, Macro-F1, intent accuracy, tool selection and argument Exact Match (EM), end-to-end pass rate, adversarial tool gating, and safe behavior under $\text{max\_tool\_calls}=1$ and a $60$-s timeout.

Scenario~2 evaluates Top-7 synthesis using Ragas~\cite{es2023ragas}: Answer Relevancy (AR), Context Recall (CR), Context Precision (CP), and Answer Correctness (AC). Factual Exact Match (Fact EM) measures strict numerical tuition-fee and credit agreement, while Source Recall measures gold-source coverage and Source Average Precision (Source AP) evaluates ranked, verified regulatory citations. To limit LLM-judge bias~\cite{zheng2023judging,es2023ragas}, evaluation conditions reference-grounded metrics on gold documents, uses greedy decoding over three runs, and includes programmatic Fact EM for tuition arithmetic.

\subsection{Experimental Scenarios and Benchmark Results}
\label{sec:scenarios}

We evaluate \system{} through retrieval component stacking (RQ3), end-to-end generation and ablation (RQ2), and multi-agent coordination and tool reliability (RQ1).

\subsubsection{Scenario 1 --- Retrieval Component Stacking (E1--E5):}
Scenario~1 compares five cumulative retrieval configurations, from sparse and dense baselines to hybrid fusion, neural reranking, and the proposed intent-guided evidence stack, at aggregate and complexity-stratified levels on the 100-query held-out set.

Table~\ref{tab:retrieval_evaluation} shows that E5 obtains the highest aggregate retrieval scores: 0.7500 Hit@1, 0.9100 Hit@3, 0.2380 Precision@5, 0.8458 Recall@5, and 0.8354 MRR@10. Relative to E4, E5 increases Hit@1 by 7.0 percentage points and MRR@10 by 0.0450; relative to E1, its Hit@1 is 22.0 points higher. Mean retrieval latency increases from 15.62~s for E4 to 17.09~s for E5. Because E5 jointly adds intent governance, graph grounding, and parent--child mapping, this contrast compares the integrated bundle rather than any isolated component.

\subsubsection{Scenario 2 --- End-to-End QA Generation and Modular Ablation (T1--T7):}
Scenario~2 compares the full system (T4) with three single-agent RAG baselines (T1--T3) and three ablations (T5--T7) over $2,100$ evaluation turns. As shown in Table~\ref{tab:e2e_evaluation}, T4 exceeds BM25 on all seven metrics, including a 7.33-percentage-point difference in Fact~EM ($66.07\%$ vs.\ $58.74\%$). T4 records the highest Fact~EM and Source AP and ties T6 on Source Recall; T6 leads Answer Relevancy and Answer Correctness, whereas T7 leads Context Precision and Context Recall. The differences are therefore metric-specific.

\subsection{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{Answering RQ1:} Before production route repair, the evaluated raw structured supervisor achieves $97.0\%$ routing accuracy, $97.8\%$ Macro-F1, and $95.0\%$ intent accuracy, compared with $85.0\%$, $69.2\%$, and $69.0\%$ for rule-based routing. The deterministic tuition tools achieve $100.0\%$ end-to-end pass rates, whereas scholarship calculation reaches $76.7\%$ with $86.7\%$ tool selection; all tools maintain $100.0\%$ bounded completion. Adversarial tests obtain $100.0\%$ tool-gating accuracy, $98.3\%$ safe-result behavior, and $100.0\%$ bounded completion. In a paired stress-test with 11 tools, single-agent and multi-agent end-to-end performance is $93.3\%$ and $88.3\%$, respectively, without a statistically significant difference. After adding 16 synthetic distractor tools (27 tools total), monolithic tool selection decreases from $98.3\%$ to $91.7\%$, whereas the domain-partitioned system maintains $100.0\%$ (paired $\Delta=+8.3$ percentage points, 95\% bootstrap CI $[+1.7,+15.0]$, $n=60$). These results suggest that partitioned tool access may reduce decision-space interference as the evaluated registry expands.

\textbf{Answering RQ2:} T4 exceeds the no-cross-reranker configuration T5 on every reported metric, supporting the contribution of cross-reranking in this setting. Comparisons with T6 and T7 are mixed: T4 leads T6 in Context Recall, Source AP, and Fact~EM but trails it in Answer Relevancy and Answer Correctness and ties it on Source Recall. T4 leads T7 in Answer Relevancy, Answer Correctness, Source Recall, Source AP, and Fact~EM but trails it in Context Precision and Context Recall. The ablations therefore indicate component-specific associations rather than uniform causal effects.

\textbf{Answering RQ3:} E4 improves both early-rank ordering and evidence coverage over E3, while E5 provides smaller aggregate gains. E5's largest Hit@1 differences over E4 occur on entity-comparison, temporal, and cross-domain queries. Direct and within-domain multi-hop queries show smaller or no Hit@1 gains, and the adversarial tier remains difficult for both configurations. E5 also has slightly lower cross-domain Recall@5 despite its higher Hit@1. Because E5 changes three components jointly, these results do not isolate their individual causal effects.

%------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Threats to Validity and Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The evaluation is limited to CTU and 100 held-out queries; the temporal and adversarial tiers contain only five each. Domain analysis covers 80 single-domain queries and excludes 20 cross-domain cases. Using Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite as generator and judge may introduce bias despite grounding and repeated deterministic runs; judges may also penalize valid abstentions. Scenario~3 isolates raw routing and one-decision gates rather than the production repair/retry path. Curriculum graphs use offline schemas, while live Neo4j/Qdrant fault injection and multi-institutional validation remain future work.
