\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study developed and evaluated \system{}, a supervisor-routed multi-agent RAG framework for heterogeneous university counseling, through architecture-level comparison, mechanistic ablation, and tool-ambiguity stress testing.

Three principal findings address our research questions. For RQ1, supervisor-routed multi-agent decomposition obtains an 8.0\,pp higher End-to-End Success point estimate (81.0\% vs.\ 73.0\%) and 41.4\% fewer input tokens than a functionally matched single-agent baseline, with an associated latency trade-off. For RQ2, ablation reveals that specialist policies ($-10.0$\,pp E2E) and heterogeneous evidence paths ($-12.3$\,pp Source Recall) are the components associated with the largest observed degradations, with structured-financial and cross-domain queries degrading most under uniform text retrieval. For RQ3, the Routed Specialist maintains an endpoint execution advantage at 51 tools (88.0\% vs.\ 83.3\% for Single Agent and 78.7\% for Global Top-10) without displaying statistically divergent degradation slopes.

These findings indicate that supervisor-routed bounded specialization---combining domain-specific policies, scoped tool access, representation-specific evidence paths, and deterministic calculation---supports institutional counseling reliability under the evaluated conditions, with measurable coordination trade-offs. Future work includes reducing routing overhead via parallel dispatch, extending benchmarks to bilingual settings, and conducting field trials on student advisory efficacy.
