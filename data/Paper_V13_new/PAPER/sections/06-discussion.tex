%------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Answering the Research Questions}

\textbf{RQ1 (Architecture-Level Effectiveness):}
The architecture-level comparison indicates that supervisor-routed multi-agent decomposition obtains an 8.0\,pp higher E2E point estimate than the Unified Single Agent (81.0\% vs.\ 73.0\%), although the paired bootstrap interval touches zero ($[0.0, +16.0]$\,pp; McNemar $p{=}0.096$). CTU-Chat also attains higher continuous Fact Coverage ($\Delta{=}+0.028$, 95\%\,CI $[-0.021,+0.079]$) and 41.4\% fewer input tokens (3{,}690 vs.\ 6{,}296), alongside a median latency trade-off (10{,}652 vs.\ 5{,}159\,ms). The Routed Generic configuration (59.6\% Fact Coverage) demonstrates that routing without specialist policies impairs performance ($\Delta{=}-0.071$ vs.\ CTU-Chat, $p{<}0.05$), indicating that multi-agent decomposition is beneficial only when paired with policy specialization.

\textbf{RQ2 (Architectural Mechanism):}
The ablations support the hypothesis that the architectural components serve distinct functions:
(1)~Replacing representation-specific access with uniform text retrieval (S2-A3) produces the steepest Source Recall drop ($-12.3$\,pp), with structured-financial queries suffering most ($-17.8$\,pp);
(2)~Removing specialist prompts (S2-A1) causes the largest Fact Coverage drop ($-7.05$\,pp);
(3)~Full tool visibility (S2-A2) inflates tokens by $+1{,}270$ without improving fact coverage, confirming tool isolation as a cost-control mechanism;
(4)~Omitting route repair (S2-A5) degrades cross-domain queries by $-15.0$\,pp;
(5)~Disabling deterministic calculation (S2-A4) causes $-6.0$\,pp E2E loss on the full benchmark and a 55.6\,pp pass degradation on the arithmetic tuition subset.

\textbf{RQ3 (Tool Ambiguity):}
As registries scale from 11 to 51 tools, the Routed Specialist preserves 88.0\% E2E with 9.8 visible tools. The Full-Registry Single Agent degrades from 86.0\% to 83.3\% while expanding tokens by 124\%; the Global Top-10 drops to 78.7\% (coinciding with shortlist truncation). These observations indicate that scoped tool registries preserve endpoint execution reliability at 51 tools, though registry expansion did not induce statistically divergent degradation slopes.

\subsection{Relation to Prior Work}

Our findings connect to several active themes. While ReAct~\cite{yao2023react} and Toolformer~\cite{schick2023toolformer} demonstrate tool execution in single-agent settings, our results show that unconstrained registries suffer token inflation and elevated tool confusion under semantic distractors. General multi-agent frameworks such as AutoGen~\cite{wu2023autogen} permit flexible inter-agent interaction; CTU-Chat instead adopts strictly hierarchical, contract-validated routing with single-owner prefetch. Our ablation (S2-A3) aligns with findings in GraphRAG~\cite{edge2024graphrag}, indicating that replacing representation-specific access with uniform text retrieval substantially reduces source recall in the evaluated benchmark. Unlike Adaptive-RAG~\cite{jeong2024adaptiverag}, which routes by semantic complexity, CTU-Chat routes by institutional representation requirements.

\subsection{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limitations}
\textbf{Internal:} Single LLM (Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite); while temperature-zero decoding minimizes sampling variance, minor API-serving non-determinism and prompt sensitivity remain potential confounds.
\textbf{External:} Single-institution benchmark (100 queries, 5 temporal, 5 adversarial); results may not generalize to other regulatory structures. Semantic-neighbor tools are evaluation constructs.
\textbf{Construct:} E2E metrics rely partly on Ragas automated evaluation; required-fact matching reduces generator--judge bias. Human evaluation on a subset would strengthen validity.
\textbf{Statistical:} Bootstrap CIs are query-level ($N{=}100$). The arithmetic ablation subset is small ($N{=}9$), though exact counts show 8/9 exact matches with calculator vs.\ 3/9 without. Three registry sizes do not support asymptotic scaling claims.
\textbf{Operational:} Neural reranking was evaluated on dedicated GPU hardware. Wall-clock latency includes API and network serving variability and should therefore be interpreted as deployment-specific.
