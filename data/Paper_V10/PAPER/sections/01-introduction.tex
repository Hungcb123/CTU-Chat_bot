\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Can Tho University (CTU) is a key higher education institution in the Mekong Delta, with nearly 49,000 undergraduate students in the 2025--2026 academic year~\cite{ctu2025opening}. At an institution of this scale, advisory needs span a wide range of academic and administrative areas. Relevant regulations and policies may also change over time. Prior research on automated advisory systems includes CAAS, which supports chat- and voice-based admission counseling for information-technology programs~\cite{ma2023caas}, and REBot, which focuses on academic-regulation advising through dense retrieval and category-guided graph reasoning~\cite{ma2025rebot}. Their reported evaluations illustrate the value of task-specific retrieval and processing strategies, but remain centered on individual advisory domains. This motivates CTU-Chat as a coordinated multi-domain architecture for heterogeneous university counseling.

University counseling presents challenges not only because it covers multiple domains, but also because institutional knowledge is represented in different forms. (1)~\textbf{Academic curricula} contain hierarchical structures and prerequisite relationships; (2)~\textbf{tuition schedules} are organized by admission cohort and field of study; (3)~\textbf{tuition exemption policies} and (4)~\textbf{scholarship policies} combine eligibility conditions with specific support rates and thresholds; while (5)~\textbf{general administrative regulations} are presented in lengthy narrative documents. Therefore, a single representation or retrieval method may not be equally suitable across all domains, motivating representation-specific evidence acquisition in CTU-Chat.

RAG and GraphRAG augment LLMs with external documents and knowledge graphs~\cite{lewis2020rag,gao2023rag_survey,edge2024graphrag}, while agentic architectures support tool invocation and multi-agent coordination~\cite{yao2023react,wu2023autogen,schick2023toolformer}. However, these approaches do not by themselves determine how heterogeneous institutional evidence should be represented, routed, and processed. Moreover, known limitations in language-model arithmetic~\cite{qian2023limitations} motivate deterministic calculation for rule-based financial queries.

These challenges highlight three open research gaps in automated university counseling: (1) limited domain-specialized multi-agent orchestration; (2) uniform representation of heterogeneous institutional knowledge; and (3) the lack of representation-aware evidence routing. These gaps are examined in greater detail in Section~2.

The objective of this study is to develop and evaluate CTU-Chat for heterogeneous university counseling. To address the identified gaps, we formulate three research questions:
\begin{itemize}
    \setlength{\itemsep}{1.5pt}
    \setlength{\parsep}{0pt}
    \setlength{\parskip}{0pt}

    \item \textbf{RQ1 (Domain-Specialized Tool Disambiguation and Scalability):}
    How do full-registry and Top-$k$ single-agent baselines compare with supervisor-routed generic and specialist gates in tool-selection reliability, end-to-end completion, and computational cost as semantically overlapping within-domain tools expand the registry from 11 to 51 tools?

    \item \textbf{RQ2 (Heterogeneous Knowledge Allocation \& Modular Ablations):}
    What differences in context and answer quality are observed between the full heterogeneous configuration, uniform retrieval baselines, and its component ablations?

    \item \textbf{RQ3 (Representation-Aware Retrieval Effectiveness):}
    How does the complete representation-aware evidence configuration compare with sparse, dense, hybrid, and reranked baselines in retrieval effectiveness, both overall and across counseling domains?
\end{itemize}

We hypothesize that domain-scoped candidate retrieval combined with contrastive specialist policies can reduce confusion among semantically adjacent tools relative to a matched generic single-agent gate. This hypothesis concerns tool disambiguation under the evaluated registry expansions; it does not assume that multi-agent execution will dominate a full-registry monolithic baseline on every accuracy or efficiency measure. To investigate this hypothesis and the research questions above, we propose CTU-Chat, which coordinates four domain-specialized agents for academic, financial, scholarship, and general administrative inquiries within a unified framework combining graph-based retrieval, structured data access, deterministic calculation, and hybrid document retrieval.

Specifically, this work provides three primary contributions:
\begin{itemize}
    \setlength{\itemsep}{1.5pt}
    \setlength{\parsep}{0pt}
    \setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \item \textbf{C1 (Domain-Specialized Multi-Agent Architecture):} We design and implement a centralized supervisor-routed architecture coordinating four bounded specialists with isolated evidence representations, deterministic route repair, and partitioned tool privileges, and evaluate its tool-disambiguation behavior in registries of up to 51 tools.
    \item \textbf{C2 (Heterogeneous Knowledge Allocation):} We develop a hybrid representation strategy that models relational curricula via property graphs, binds statutory fee and exemption lookup to deterministic arithmetic tools, and indexes narrative regulations through parent-resolved document retrieval.
    \item \textbf{C3 (Empirical Scalability and End-to-End Evaluation):} We evaluate semantic-neighbor tool ambiguity across 1,960 decisions with matched baselines, retrieval component stacking across 5 configurations, and generation quality across 7 configurations ($2,100$ turns) on a redesigned 100-query held-out benchmark.
\end{itemize}

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. \textbf{Section~2} reviews related work and summarizes the research gaps. \textbf{Section~3} presents the CTU-Chat architecture, evidence-acquisition methods, and domain-specialized agents. \textbf{Section~4} reports the experimental setup, results, and discussion. Finally, \textbf{Section~5} concludes the paper and outlines future work.
