\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study examined centralized supervision and representation-specific evidence paths for heterogeneous university counseling. Raw supervisor output reaches 97.0\% agent accuracy. In the semantic-neighbor study, the routed specialist is the only evaluated configuration whose decision-level end-to-end pass rate improves between the 11- and 51-tool endpoints ($85.3\% \rightarrow 88.0\%$, $\Delta=+2.7$~pp), while the full-registry single agent declines by 2.7~pp and the Top-10 single agent by 4.6~pp. At 51 tools, the specialist exceeds the Top-10 single-agent baseline in decision-level end-to-end pass rate by 9.3~pp (95\% CI $[+2.0,+17.3]$) and uses 52.4\% fewer input tokens than the full-registry monolithic baseline ($1{,}770$ vs.\ $3{,}722$). The high-minus-low interaction estimates are positive for selection ($+6.0$~pp) and decision-level end-to-end pass rate ($+7.3$~pp), but their confidence intervals include zero; larger samples are needed to determine whether the degradation rates differ. Comparisons with the routed generic gate are consistent with a possible contribution from contrastive domain policies. On the 100-query held-out set, E5 records 0.7400 Hit@1, 0.8608 Recall@5, and 0.8333 MRR@10, while T4 leads Context Recall (0.6534), Faithfulness (0.8636), Source Recall (0.8325), and Source AP (0.7316); individual ablations lead other metrics.

These findings suggest that domain-partitioned routing with contrastive specialist gates can support tool disambiguation at the evaluated registry sizes. The fixed-level results favor the specialist over the Top-10 single-agent baseline in decision-level end-to-end pass rate at 51 tools, whereas the scaling-rate comparison remains inconclusive. Heterogeneous evidence may also benefit from representation-specific paths. The routed specialist incurs approximately $1.6$--$2.0\times$ the decision latency of the single-agent configurations across the tested registry sizes. Future work will use anonymized logs and human and cross-institutional evaluation to test learned candidate retrieval, compressed routing, independent routes, and larger samples under naturally evolving tool registries.
