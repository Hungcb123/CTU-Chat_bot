\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study evaluated centralized supervision and representation-specific evidence paths for university counseling. At 51 tools, the routed specialist has a $+9.3$-pp decision-level E2E difference from Top-10 single (95\% CI $[+2.0,+17.3]$), but uses 70.8\% more input tokens and $2.03\times$ the latency. Against full-registry single, it uses 52.4\% fewer input tokens; its $+4.7$-pp E2E difference has a CI containing zero. Positive high-minus-low interactions also touch zero, so the study establishes a fixed-level advantage over Top-10 at 51 tools, not a scaling-rate advantage. On the 100-query held-out set, E5 records 0.7400 Hit@1, 0.8608 Recall@5, and 0.8333 MRR@10; T4 leads four of eight generation metrics, while ablations lead the others. These findings suggest that scoped specialist policies and heterogeneous evidence paths can support the evaluated tasks, subject to accuracy--cost trade-offs. Future work will test compressed routing, independent routes, larger natural tool registries, and human and cross-institutional evaluation.
