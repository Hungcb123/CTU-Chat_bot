\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study examined centralized supervision and representation-specific evidence paths for heterogeneous university counseling. Raw supervisor output reaches 97.0\% agent accuracy. In the semantic-neighbor study, the routed specialist is the only architecture whose end-to-end pass rate improves as the registry scales from 11 to 51 tools ($85.3\% \rightarrow 88.0\%$, $\Delta=+2.7$~pp), while the full-registry single agent degrades by 2.7~pp and the Top-10 single agent by 4.6~pp over the same range. At 51 tools, the specialist exceeds the matched Top-10 single agent in end-to-end completion by 9.3~pp (95\% CI $[+2.0,+17.3]$) and consumes 52.4\% fewer input tokens than the full-registry monolithic baseline ($1{,}770$ vs.\ $3{,}722$). The high-minus-low scaling interaction is positive for both selection ($+6.0$~pp) and end-to-end completion ($+7.3$~pp), with confidence intervals approaching but not excluding zero; replication with larger samples is warranted. Comparisons with the routed generic gate are consistent with a contribution from contrastive domain policies. On the 100-query held-out set, E5 records 0.7400 Hit@1, 0.8608 Recall@5, and 0.8333 MRR@10, while T4 leads Context Recall (0.6534), Faithfulness (0.8636), Source Recall (0.8325), and Source AP (0.7316); individual ablations lead other metrics.

These findings suggest that domain-partitioned routing with contrastive specialist gates can maintain or improve both accuracy and token efficiency as the tool registry grows under the evaluated conditions, while monolithic and unstructured Top-$k$ approaches tend to degrade under increasing semantic-neighbor density. Heterogeneous evidence may benefit from representation-specific paths. The routed specialist incurs approximately $2\times$ higher latency than single-agent alternatives, and the scaling interaction, while positive, has not been confirmed at larger sample sizes. Future work will use anonymized logs and human and cross-institutional evaluation to test learned candidate retrieval, compressed routing, and independent routes at larger scales.
